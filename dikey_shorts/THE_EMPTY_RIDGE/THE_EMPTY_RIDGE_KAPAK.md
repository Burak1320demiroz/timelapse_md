## 📸 KAPAK GÖRSEL PROMPTU (G7 + başlık bandı satırı)

```text
Photorealistic documentary construction photograph — master cover frame, derived from G7. Vertical time-lapse of a single wind turbine erected on a bare grass spur above a wide valley, seen from a higher ridge to the south; a construction-progress photograph from a fixed 24 mm site camera, film grain.
Lens: 24mm spherical, f/8, ISO 100.
Camera: MAIN STATION — fixed tripod bolted to a rock outcrop on the south ridge, lens 100.0 metres above the turbine pad, 320 metres from the tower axis, bearing 350 degrees, tilt 0 degrees, zero roll. The camera never moves.

STRUCTURE: When finished this will be a single white three-bladed wind turbine on a tubular steel tower standing on the crest of the spur. AT THIS STAGE: the turbine is complete and turning, and the site is cleared. On the axis the white tower carries the nacelle and its three-bladed rotor facing the camera, the rotor turning slowly so that the blade tips carry a faint motion blur, one blade near the top of its sweep above the far skyline. The crushed-stone pad left of the tower lies empty, with crawler track marks, timber blocks and the pale scar of the road as the only signs of the work, and the windsock hangs slack. The field has been ploughed into dark brown strips between bands of stubble, and the oak's crown shows bronze at the edges.

SITE / BACKGROUND: The grass crest of the spur runs level across the frame a little below one third of the frame height; on it, right of centre, stands one lone oak with a broad dark crown, and left of centre a low drystone sheepfold wall runs along the crest. Below the crest a single wheat field fills the near flank to the bottom edge. Beyond the crest the ground falls to a wide hazy valley closed by a long mountain range whose skyline runs level a little above half the frame height; above it, the sky. The eye line at exactly half the frame height runs along the lower slopes of the far range.

THE TIME MOMENT: The rotor turns on its own above the empty pad, the field ploughed — the machine is running and the spur has a new tallest thing.

COMPOSITION: Vertical 9:16. The spur crest runs level at about twenty-nine percent of frame height, the eye line at exactly half the frame height runs along the lower slopes of the far range, and the far skyline at about fifty-seven percent. The lone oak spans about 64 pixels of frame height — one thirtieth — and keeps that height and position in every frame. Tower and nacelle rise from twenty-nine to about fifty-one percent on the axis and the turning blades sweep a circle from about forty-three to sixty-five percent, its top above the far skyline; the empty pad lies left of the tower. The tallest part of the structure sits below the top eighth, the working level above the bottom quarter, and nothing that proves progress falls into the right eighth.

LIGHTING: Sun azimuth 296 degrees, elevation 7 degrees, sunset light, very long golden shadows falling to the east-south-east, toward the right of the frame, the tower's shadow reaching along the crest past the oak. The sun stays outside the frame for the whole shot; its only sign is the shadows. Late September: clear warm sky with thin cirrus, the far range in warm side light, the valley haze golden.

TIME RATE: Everything in this frame is time-lapsed at the same rate: clouds, shadows, grass, machines and trucks all move at the same accelerated speed. Two hours pass across this shot: the shadows on the crest sweep across the ground, high cirrus stretches into thin streaks across the sky, and every moving machine smears into heavy motion blur. Nothing in the frame moves at normal speed.

ACTIVITY: The rotor turns slowly clockwise as seen from the camera, its three blade tips smeared into faint arcs; a tractor pulls a plough along the stubble and turns a fresh dark strip of earth, leaving tyre ruts and a dust haze behind it, and a pickup truck crawls down the road as a colour trail. Crew register only as faint soft motion streaks beside their machines, never as sharp figures; a parked truck stays sharp.

SITE TEXTURE: The tower, nacelle and blades smooth white painted steel with bolted flange bands; the empty pad pale crushed stone with crawler track marks, tyre ruts, timber blocks and a dust film; the road gravel with ruts and grassed-over spoil windrows; the field dark brown ploughed strips with a moist sheen between pale stubble bands; the drystone wall grey lichen-spotted stone.

PHYSICS: The rotor turns on the hub, the hub is bolted to the nacelle, the nacelle sits bolted on the tower, and the tower bears on the foundation as one rigid machine. The tractor stands on its tyres in the field with the plough shares pressed into the earth; the pickup stands on its tyres on the road; the windsock pole is planted in its footing.

NEGATIVE: Locked-off camera on a fixed tripod — the camera never pans, tilts, zooms or drifts; the framing is identical from the first frame to the last. The spur crest, the lone oak, the drystone wall, the valley and the far skyline never change shape, position, size or spacing. The turbine is one rigid steel structure that only ever gains parts — nothing already built is removed, moved or resized; no opacity fades, no fading into existence. No readable text, letters, digits, numbers or symbols anywhere in the frame, on any surface: machinery, truck, licence plate, signage, hoarding, container, helmet, vest included. Nothing here has been branded, labelled, numbered or annotated by anyone. No cuts, no dissolves, no fades, no transitions. No architectural visualisation look, no CGI aesthetic, no miniature or toy look, no tilt-shift blur. Photorealistic documentary photography, 35mm film grain.
Leave the band between roughly seventy-two and eighty-four percent of frame height visually simple across the middle of the picture: open sky with thin cirrus, no strong edge and no bright object, so that a title can be set over it in post-production.
Output: 9:16, 4K, no text, no watermark.
```
