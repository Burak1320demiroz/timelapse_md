## 📸 KAPAK GÖRSEL PROMPTU (G6 + başlık bandı ton satırı)

```text
Photorealistic documentary construction photograph — master cover frame, derived from G6. Vertical time-lapse of a single granite lighthouse built on a bare sea rock offshore, seen from a neighbouring sea stack to the south; a construction-progress photograph from a fixed 24 mm site camera.
Lens: 24mm spherical, f/8, ISO 100.
Camera: MAIN STATION — fixed tripod bolted to the flat summit of the south stack, lens 21.0 metres above mean sea level, 56 metres from the tower axis, bearing 0 degrees, tilt 0 degrees, zero roll. The camera never moves.

STRUCTURE: When finished this will be a single lighthouse of unpainted pale-grey granite standing on the rock, with a gallery and a copper-roofed lantern on top. AT THIS STAGE: the lighthouse is complete, its light is on, and the rock is cleared. On the cut plinth the round tapering tower of pale-grey granite rises to its gallery and lantern, left bare and unpainted, the same pale grey from base to gallery. Inside the lantern the lamp is lit: the glass panes glow with a steady warm-white light that stays inside the glass, under the new copper roof and its wind vane at about eighty-two percent of frame height. The apron beside the tower is clean empty rock with a paler square patch where the hoist mast stood, and the orange landing ramp stays seated in its socket, running down the steps to the calm water as the lighthouse's permanent landing.

SITE / BACKGROUND: A single dark sea rock rises from the water in the middle of the frame, its lower flanks wet with a brown seaweed band at the waterline. To its right and farther out stands a single sea stack, the needle, a narrow dark column with a white streak on its top. Behind them open sea runs to a perfectly level horizon; on the left a long flat-topped headland three kilometres away rises a little above it, with one pale field on its slope. Apart from the work on the rock, the headland, the sea and the needle carry only bare rock, grass and water from edge to edge. The eye line at exactly half the frame height runs along the sea horizon.

THE TIME MOMENT: The light is on for the first time above a cleared rock at the last of the daylight — the cut top has become a base, the landing stays, and the tower is working.

COMPOSITION: Vertical 9:16. The sea horizon runs level at exactly half the frame height and the needle stands on the right between about thirty-four and forty-five percent. The needle spans about 200 pixels of frame height — one tenth — and keeps that height and position in every frame. The glowing lantern sits between about seventy-three and seventy-seven percent and the wind vane tip at about eighty-two percent on the centre line; the empty apron and the ramp head sit at about thirty percent on the left. The crown of the structure sits below the top eighth of the frame and the working level sits above the bottom quarter of the frame; nothing that proves progress falls into the right eighth of the frame.

LIGHTING: Sun azimuth 290 degrees, elevation 1 degree, the last direct light of sunset from far left, very long shadows falling to the east-south-east, toward the right of the frame, the tower's west face warm gold and its east face in blue shade. The sun stays outside the frame for the whole shot; its only sign is the shadows. Evening at the end of summer: the sky golden low on the left and deepening blue overhead with thin cirrus, the sea dark slate and silky, the rock darker still, so the lit lantern is the brightest thing in the frame.

TIME RATE: Everything in this frame is time-lapsed at the same rate: clouds, shadows and the sea foam all move at the same accelerated speed. Two hours pass across this shot: the long shadows sweep across the rock, thin cirrus stretches into streaks, and the sea smooths to dark silk with a thin line of white foam around the rock. Nothing in the frame moves at normal speed.

ACTIVITY: The lamp inside the lantern burns with a steady warm-white glow; thin cirrus streaks slowly across the golden sky and a thin line of foam drifts around the rock. Crew register only as faint soft motion streaks — a single one on the gallery beside the lantern — never as sharp figures.

SITE TEXTURE: The granite pale grey warmed by the low sun, with fine level joint lines and faint rust streaks where the scaffold ties were; the copper roof bright and new; the empty apron clean pale rock with a paler square patch and old track marks under a film of stone dust; salt stains along the orange ramp; the rock below dark with its brown seaweed band; a thin band of white foam on the dark silky sea.

PHYSICS: The lantern frame is bolted to the gallery floor and the copper roof bears on it; every granite course bears on the course below and the whole tower is founded on the cut plinth; the landing ramp is seated in its socket and chained to rings in the rock.

NEGATIVE: Locked-off camera on a fixed tripod — the camera never pans, tilts, zooms or drifts; the framing is identical from the first frame to the last. The lower flanks of the rock, the needle, the sea horizon and the headland never change shape, position, size or spacing; the sea level against the rock never changes. The lighthouse is one rigid stone structure that only ever gains courses and parts — nothing already built is removed, moved or resized; no opacity fades, no fading into existence. No readable text, letters, digits, numbers or symbols anywhere in the frame, on any surface: machinery, hull, scaffolding, hoist mast, glass panel, facade, signage, helmet, vest included. Nothing here has been branded, labelled, numbered or annotated by anyone. No cuts, no dissolves, no fades, no transitions. No architectural visualisation look, no CGI aesthetic, no miniature or toy look, no tilt-shift blur.
Keep the lower band between roughly twenty-seven and forty-five percent of frame height dark and low in contrast across the whole width: dark slate water, the dark rock and the needle in shade, with no bright highlight and no foam glare in that band, so that a light title can be set over it in post-production.
Output: 9:16, 4K, no text, no watermark.
```
