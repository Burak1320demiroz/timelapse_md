## 📝 MÜZİK PROMPTU [§1.4 iskeletinin 160 sn · 20 × 8 sn hâli]

```
Instrumental piece for a 160-second horizontal restoration time-lapse: twenty 8-second shots, no end card.
No lyrics, no voice, no traditional song structure. It must sit under the site sounds until 144 s.

Genre: warm cinematic chamber score, piano and strings, reaching a gentle main theme at the end.
Tempo: 120 BPM, strict and bar-aligned, four bars per 8-second shot.

Layers:
1. [shot 4] a soft low pulse on the first beat of every bar
2. [shot 5] a low sustained bass note
3. [shot 6] a simple four-note motif on a single piano voice — the identity of the piece
4. [shots 7-11] a wide warm string pad; the motif returns a little fuller
   [shot 12] everything steps back to layer 2 alone, very low, leaving room for three short, solid accents
   [shots 13-15] layers 1-4 return, a little warmer and closer in shot 15
   [shot 16] back to layer 2 alone, very low; the room is left to wind
   [shots 17-18] layer 2 with the motif once more, soft, on one piano voice
5. [shots 19-20] the motif becomes the full main theme, strings and piano, warm and unhurried; in shot 20 it reaches
   its peak and holds

Dynamic map across 160 seconds:
  0.0-24.0     silence — only the site's own sound (wind and the crane's motor from the first frame, a taut sling's
               low creak, bolts, old panes lifted out)
  24.0-32.0    layer 1 enters, very low
  32.0-40.0    layer 2 enters
  40.0-48.0    layer 3: the motif, first time
  48.0-88.0    layer 4 enters and grows; the motif a second time, fuller
  88.0-96.0    the score lightens to layer 2 alone; three short, solid, quiet accents fall where they are cued
  96.0-120.0   layers 1-4 return; slightly closer and warmer from 112 s
  120.0-128.0  the score drops to layer 2 alone, almost inaudible; wind takes over
  128.0-144.0  layer 2 and the motif, soft, on one piano voice
  144.0-152.0  the main theme begins, strings and piano
  152.0-160.0  the main theme at its peak; it softens in the last 1.5 seconds — no hard cut

Phone-speaker rule: every sub-bass element below 150 Hz is doubled by its own upper harmonics at roughly 100, 150 and
200 Hz so the fundamental is implied rather than required; the low end is never the only carrier of the weight.
```
