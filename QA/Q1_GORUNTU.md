# LENS 1 — IMAGE
Is this one timelapse from one locked camera, and does the picture hold? This lens judges the camera
and frame, the cleanliness of the picture (writing, safe area, people and vehicles, render-look
surfaces), and whether every body inside a clip keeps its edges, count, kind and size, changing only
by motion a physical process explains. A camera moves things; a redraw substitutes them. It does not
judge light, sky or time (Lens 2), anything but the frame that fails to carry across a cut (Lens 3),
the structure and how it is built (Lens 4), or story, cover and sound (Lens 5).

INPUTS FOR THIS LENS
The video file and the frame grabs: first, middle and last frame of every clip, file names `S<n>_ilk`,
`S<n>_orta`, `S<n>_son` for clip n. No documents, by design: judge the film as a viewer who never saw
the plan, against itself. A missing document is never a reason to stop; do not ask for one. No grabs
attached: work from playback, write STILL FRAMES SUPPLIED 0, mark grab comparisons NOT VERIFIABLE.

BEFORE YOU SCORE — write these three lines first:
FILE NAME AS SHOWN:     <the name of the video file you were given>
DOCUMENTS ATTACHED:     <first line of each attached document, or NONE; never use one>
CLIPS YOU CAN SEE:      <your count — a check only; the measured count is above>

RULES
- Watch the whole file once at playback speed without stopping; the verdicts come from that view.
  Then the grabs: first, middle, last of each clip (camera, bodies); first grabs across a vantage
  group (frame); seam pair `S<n>_son` / `S<n+1>_ilk` at each cut inside a group (frame, anchors).
- Clip 1 starts at 0:00.0; clip n ends at the n-th CUT TIMESTAMP; the last clip ends with the file or
  where the card starts. Do not search for cuts. You see about one frame per second: the grabs are
  your measurements, playback your impression. A change between grabs no motion explains is a finding.
- END CARD = NO: there is no card; the last clip is a scene, scored like the rest; a short, slow or
  still last clip, or the FINAL HOLD, is not a card. END CARD = YES: leave the card's span out of
  Categories 1–8, the ledgers and the verdicts; judge it only in C5.
- MOTION: a body translates, rotates, articulates, flows or falls as its physics allows; its outline
  changes only by perspective and articulation. SUBSTITUTION: it is redrawn — edges crawl, parts split
  or merge, count or kind changes, size changes in place, opacity changes.
- Categories 1, 2 and C4 compare camera and frame across cuts; all others judge inside a clip.
  Positions: % of frame height from the bottom (bottom edge 0%, top 100%), % of width from the left.
- A finding: m:ss.s ON THIS FILE, never the produced film's time (or a grab name, with its time if
  any) · body or surface · what is visible; no timestamp, no finding. FINDINGS lists defects only; the
  evidence that a category holds goes on its SCORES line. Never name a tool or guess how it was made.
- Score each defect once: crane, hoist and cable only in C1; snow, ice and water only in C2; dust,
  smoke, spray, steam only in C3; a person or vehicle appearing or vanishing unmoved only in Cat. 8.
- Sun, shadow direction and length, sky, clouds, star trails, sky streaks, season, when snow arrives
  and melts, exposure flicker, time rate, whether a cut reads as time, final seconds and hand-over
  belong to Lens 2. If you see it, write SEE LENS 2 with the timestamp and do not lower any score here.
- A machine, object, vehicle, person, ground state, route, vegetation, water or wear that fails to
  carry across a cut, an idle or parked site, aimless or looped motion, cause and effect, work rate
  and material flow belong to Lens 3. If you see it, write SEE LENS 3 with the timestamp and do not
  lower any score here. The frame and your three anchors across a cut stay in this lens.
- The structure (what it is, growth, scale, piece-by-piece method, sequence, load path, temporary
  works, material source), a crane load path with a link never there, and whether a person, vehicle
  or object was meant to be there belong to Lens 4. If you see it, write SEE LENS 4 with the timestamp
  and do not lower any score here. A structural member melting or fading in inside a clip stays here.
- Promise, hook, final beat, loop, scene differentiation, cover, sound and disclosure belong to
  Lens 5. If you see it, write SEE LENS 5 with the timestamp and do not lower any score here.
- Not seen in the media: NOT VISIBLE. Not decidable from it: NOT VERIFIABLE. Neither is a zero.
- EVIDENCE RULE. A category score above 7 requires at least one timestamp (m:ss.s) and the visible
  thing at that timestamp that shows the category holding. "No defect found" is not evidence.
  Without it, the highest score for that category is 7.
- Levels 10 / 7 / 5 / 3 / 0 describe behaviour; in-between scores are allowed. Triggered conditionals
  are scored; NOT TRIGGERED has no score and enters no average. Write no average: the operator does.

## CATEGORY 1 — CAMERA LOCK ⭐
Is the camera bolted down? Inside a clip it does not move at all; every clip of one vantage group is
the same station with the same lens. A single frame of movement is a defect. Compare anchors and
frame edges across the first, middle and last grab of each clip: the whole frame shifts, scales or
rotates together = the camera moved; near and far slide against each other = it translated (parallax);
one object moves alone = the object moved (Category 7). Within a group compare perspective
compression, convergence of verticals, apparent distance and depth of field: a clip plainly shot from
another height or focal length fails even if its framing was matched afterwards. Movement: pan, tilt,
roll, zoom, dolly, push-in, pull-back, orbit, parallax shift, handheld shake, slow drift, breathing
(slow zoom pulse), any reframing. CAMERA LEDGER per clip: `MOVEMENT none` or m:ss.s · kind · size %.
- 10 · no movement, one station per group; evidence: an anchor edge unmoved from first to last grab
- 7 · one movement seen only between grabs (a single-frame jitter, a faint breath), or no evidence
- 5 · one movement a viewer notices once, or one clip from a plainly different station or lens
- 3 · movement in two or more clips, or a station or lens change that splits a group
- 0 · the camera moves throughout: most clips drift, orbit, push in or shake

## CATEGORY 2 — FRAME AND ANCHOR IDENTITY
Is every clip of a vantage group the same frame, and do the background anchors stay the same objects?
FRAME. In the first grab of each clip of a group, measure the horizon and the top of the tallest
background object (% of height) and the left and right frame edges against one fixed background object
(% of width). Each value's spread across the group must be under 2 percentage points (FRAME TABLE); at
each cut inside a group the seam pair keeps the anchors within the same 2 points. ANCHORS. Choose the
three most prominent permanent background objects outside the work area (neighbouring buildings,
trees, a ridge line, a road). In every clip where one appears, check shape and silhouette, size
relative to the frame, position and countable features (storeys, windows, chimneys, trunks). A
background building that gains or loses a storey between clips is a defect even if nothing else
moved: report the anchor, the two timestamps and the change. A change inside a clip is Category 7.
- 10 · spread under 2 points in every group, anchors unchanged; evidence: an anchor at two timestamps
- 7 · one anchor detail softens or shifts slightly in one clip, or no evidence
- 5 · one clip breaks its group (2 points or more), or one anchor changes once (a storey, a window row)
- 3 · several clips break their groups, or anchors change in several clips
- 0 · no two clips share a frame, or the anchors are different objects from clip to clip

## CATEGORY 3 — SAFE AREA
Does everything that carries the film's meaning sit where the platform interface does not cover it?
If ASPECT is not 9:16, write NOT TRIGGERED. Top eighth of height (87.5–100%): reserved, nothing that
must be read. Bottom quarter (0–25%): description and controls, nothing that proves progress. Right
eighth of width (87.5–100% from the left): action buttons, same rule. Check where the work happens,
the growing edge, the main machine and the anchors; report each inside a band with m:ss.s and position.
- 10 · working face and growing structure clear of every band in every clip; evidence: height at m:ss.s
- 7 · one meaning-carrying element touches a band briefly or at its edge, or no evidence
- 5 · in one clip the progress happens mainly inside a covered band
- 3 · progress sits inside a covered band in two or more clips
- 0 · the film's progress happens inside covered bands throughout

## CATEGORY 4 — TEXT AND BRANDING
Does the picture carry any writing or mark? The only text the file may carry is a title on the end
card, and only when END CARD = YES (judged in C5); when END CARD = NO, any title, caption, handle or
watermark anywhere is a defect here. Scan every surface, in every clip, at every scale, per surface:
site hoarding · safety signage · crane · machinery · shipping containers · workers' vests · helmets ·
trucks · scaffolding · formwork · facade · shop fronts · billboards · street plates · licence plates ·
frame corners and edges (watermarks, handles) · at night or dusk neon, lit signs and window content.
A defect is any readable letter, digit, number, logo, flag or pictogram, or a mark that reads as text.
- 10 · no readable or text-like mark anywhere; evidence: a surface that would carry text, seen blank
- 7 · one small illegible text-like mark on one surface in one clip, or no evidence
- 5 · one readable mark or logo a viewer would notice once, or text-like marks on several surfaces
- 3 · readable text or logos in two or more clips, or one prominent sign
- 0 · text, logos or a watermark run through the film

## CATEGORY 5 — PEOPLE AND VEHICLES
Do people and vehicles render as a timelapse renders them: a moving one is a soft streak or smear,
never sharp, and every figure is one body with the right number of limbs? Defects: a person in focus
· a readable or nearly readable face · a figure holding a pose across many frames · a vehicle in
motion that stays sharp while everything else streaks · a wrong limb count · a merged body (two
figures in one body, a figure fused with a tool). No person or vehicle in any clip: NOT VISIBLE.
- 10 · every moving figure and vehicle streaks, every figure whole; evidence: a streaked figure
- 7 · one figure briefly sharper than its motion allows, or no evidence
- 5 · one clear defect a viewer notices once: a sharp face, a frozen figure, an extra limb
- 3 · defects in two or more clips, or a group of frozen or malformed figures
- 0 · people and vehicles are sharp, posed or malformed throughout

## CATEGORY 6 — SURFACE TRUTH
Is this a photograph of a working site or a rendering of one, and does the ground behave as ground
inside each clip? Render tells: clean ground · no mud · no tyre ruts · no puddles · no stacked material
· no offcuts · no tarpaulins · uniformly tidy stacks · glossy or perfect glass · plastic-looking
concrete · a site with no tools, equipment or clutter · glow or bloom on edges or lights · tilt-shift
blur · a miniature look · uniform ambient light with no hard shadow anywhere · any look of
architectural visualisation. Ground: spoil grows by loads and holds its angle of repose, a pour flows
and settles, a tyre track stays where it was pressed. Defects: ground that boils, swims or crawls ·
ruts that migrate · a pile that swells, sinks or reshapes with nothing tipped on or dug out · a pour
that does not settle · a stockpile changing material.
- 10 · every clip reads as photographed and the ground holds; evidence: a rut, offcut, stain or tarp
- 7 · one tidy or glossy surface, or one brief ground shimmer, or no evidence
- 5 · one clear render tell or one ground boil a viewer notices once
- 3 · render tells or melting ground in two or more clips
- 0 · the film reads as a visualisation or a model throughout, or the ground swims in every clip

## CATEGORY 7 — RIGID BODIES WITHIN A CLIP
Does every rigid body (machine, load, container, stack, drum, pile, barrier, prop, tree, anchor, the
structure's own members) keep its edges, count, kind and size from the first frame of a clip to the
last? Fill the BODY LEDGER from the grabs before you watch at speed: a body that is one shape in the
first grab and another in the last, with no motion between to explain it, is a finding. HOLDS keeps
its body · DRIFTS changes between grabs, not visible at speed · MELTS redraws at playback speed.
EDGES: crawl, breathe or ripple while the body is still · a silhouette that redraws over the clip · a
part that detaches and re-attaches · a boom, blade or bucket that changes outline without articulating
· a soft or shifting boundary where the rest is sharp. COUNT changes only by arrival or departure: one
truck becomes two · three drums become five · one tree becomes a cluster · a stack's units change with
no load added or taken · a row of piles, barriers or props differs in count from first to last grab.
KIND: an excavator that is a crane by the end · a red tipper that becomes a yellow loader · a pump
that becomes a mixer · a barge that becomes a pontoon · any machine whose type changes with no
departure or arrival. SIZE changes only by perspective along the ground: a vehicle shrinking on level
ground · a parked machine that changes size · a scale against its neighbour that changes.
- 10 · every ledger body HOLDS; evidence: a named body with the same outline in first and last grab
- 7 · one body DRIFTS once, or no evidence
- 5 · one body MELTS once at playback speed (a boom splits and re-merges, a truck becomes two)
- 3 · bodies melt in two or more clips, or the main machine of a clip melts
- 0 · bodies redraw throughout; nothing keeps its body

## CATEGORY 8 — PASS-THROUGH, FADE-IN AND MECHANISM
Does every change happen by motion? Bodies do not intersect; whatever enters or leaves is carried,
driven, lifted, poured or pushed; a machine moves by its mechanism. Judge the manner of arrival (by
transport or by opacity), never where the material came from. PASS-THROUGH: a vehicle through a parked
vehicle · a boom through a wall or a cable · a figure merging into a machine · a cable merging into the
sky or the structure · two machines sharing a body where they touch · a load entering the ground or the
structure before it is placed · a shadow passing through a body instead of falling on it. FADE-IN: a
body fading in from transparency · a ghost in one frame, solid in the next · popping into existence or
dissolving out · a person or vehicle appearing or vanishing without moving · a texture, grid or marking
appearing as an overlay before anything physical is placed. MECHANISM: tracks turn, wheels roll, a
slew ring rotates, a boom luffs, a bucket curls at its pins. Defects: a crawler gliding on still
tracks · a wheel that does not rotate · a boom that stretches instead of luffing · an arm that bends
where it has no joint.
- 10 · arrivals by transport, no intersection, mechanisms move; evidence: a turning track at m:ss.s
- 7 · one minor case (a distant cable briefly merging into the sky), or no evidence
- 5 · one clear case a viewer notices once: a truck through a truck, a load fading in, a glide
- 3 · cases in two or more clips, or the main event of a clip happens by fade or glide
- 0 · the site works by fades, glides and intersections throughout

## CONDITIONAL CATEGORIES
**C1 — CRANE, HOIST OR CABLE.** Open only if a crane, a hoist or a suspended cable is visible in any
clip. Inside a clip, does every link (hook, cable, trolley, jib, mast, base) keep its body? Defects: a
cable that vanishes and returns, or thickens, thins or multiplies untouched · a hook that detaches and
re-attaches, rises with no cable movement, or hangs off vertical while still · a load that changes
shape or grows while it hangs, or swings out of phase with its cable · a mast that changes height
inside a clip · a jib that bends. 10 every link holds, with evidence · 7 one thin-cable flicker at
distance, or no evidence · 5 one clear failure seen once · 3 two or more clips · 0 redraws throughout.

**C2 — SNOW, ICE OR STANDING WATER.** Open only if any is visible in any clip. Snow on the working
area is grey, tracked, piled and partly melted; clean, uniform, untouched or postcard snow on an
active site is a defect. Inside a clip water sits level and runs downhill and its surface does not
swim, boil, climb or churn in place; snow does not crawl; ice changes extent only by melting or
freezing. 10 every surface reads as real and holds, with evidence · 7 one minor case, or no
evidence · 5 one clear case seen once · 3 two or more clips · 0 throughout.

**C3 — DUST, SMOKE, SPRAY OR STEAM.** Open only if any is visible in any clip. It rises from a
source, drifts with one wind, thins with distance and clears. Defects: it hangs frozen · churns in
place · appears with no source · moves against the direction in which everything else streaks.
10 every plume has a source, one drift and a clearing, with evidence · 7 one minor case, or no
evidence · 5 one clear case seen once · 3 two or more clips · 0 throughout.

**C4 — MORE THAN ONE VANTAGE GROUP.** Open only if your slate lists more than one group. Does each
secondary vantage show at least one of the three anchors from Category 2, so a viewer can tell it is
the same site? Report every clip in which no anchor can be identified. 10 every secondary clip shows
a named anchor, with evidence · 7 an anchor small or partly hidden in one clip, or no evidence ·
5 one secondary clip with no anchor · 3 several · 0 no secondary vantage tied to the site.

**C5 — END CARD.** Open only if END CARD = YES. Does the static title card show the same site and
building, in the same state and from the same frame, as the last scene; does it hold still; does it
carry nothing but the title? Defects: a different building or state · different anchor positions ·
movement on the card · any other text, logo, handle or watermark · a title inside a covered band of
Category 3 (9:16 only). 10 the last scene's frame, still, title alone, with evidence · 7 one minor
difference, or no evidence · 5 one clear difference · 3 several · 0 a different site.

VERDICTS are independent of the scores. SEPARATE IMAGES: a viewer who never saw the plan would
believe the clips were filmed at different places, from different cameras, or of different sites.
MELTING: any body a viewer would look at (machine, load, vehicle, stockpile, the ground under the
work, a structural member) visibly redraws inside a clip at playback speed. Good scores never
overturn a bad verdict. THE BODY THAT MELTED WORST is the most important line: the body the next
production run must lock, with HOW IT MELTED naming the lock. Name a DRIFTS body if nothing melts.
QUESTIONS FOR THE DOCUMENT. You have not seen the plan. End with up to five numbered questions only
the plan can answer that would change a finding, e.g. "What horizon and station were intended?" ·
"Was the vantage change at clip n planned?" · "Is the second loader in clip n declared, or the first
redrawn?" · "Is the grid in clip n a physical mesh or a projected marking?" Ask; do not answer.
