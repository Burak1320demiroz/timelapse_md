# LENS 2 — TIME AND CUT
This lens answers one question: does time pass in one direction at one rate, and does every cut read
as a jump in time rather than "next shot"? You judge as a viewer who never saw the plan: sun, shadow,
season, sky, the rate of time inside each clip, exposure, what each cut says about elapsed time, how
large each jump reads, and how the last seconds bring time to rest. You do not judge the camera or
rendering, the site's machines and objects, the structure, or the story's payoff.

INPUTS FOR THIS LENS

RULES
- Watch the whole film once at normal speed without stopping, as a first-time viewer, noting which
  cuts feel like "later" and which like "next". Then go clip by clip (clip 1 runs from 0:00.0 to the
  first cut; the last clip from the last cut to the end, or to the card if END CARD = YES). Then, at
  each cut, compare the last second before it with the first second after it.
- The cut list is CUT TIMESTAMPS. Search for no other cuts, drop none, and do not judge how a cut is
  made (hard cut, dissolve, flash or black frame); that was measured. A time jump not on the list is a
  contradiction: report it. If CUT TIMESTAMPS reads NOT MEASURED, build no list of your own: write NOT
  VERIFIABLE in the CUT TABLE, for Categories 6 and 7, for C1, and for the two cut verdict lines.
- The file plays at {SPEED}x and is complete: not cut short, not ending early. Everything runs {SPEED}
  times faster than produced; that is not a time-rate defect: judge rates against each other inside
  the frame. If END CARD = NO, the last clip is a scene; a short or still last clip is not a card.
- You sample about one frame per second. Do not claim frame-to-frame flicker, exact angles or exact
  durations you cannot see. An estimated angle is labelled (est.) and names the object measured.
- Camera movement, a shifted frame or horizon, a change of vantage, readable text or logos (lit signs
  and window content included), people and vehicle rendering, render-look surfaces, and a rigid body
  melting, morphing, passing through or fading in within a clip belong to Lens 1. If you see it, write
  SEE LENS 1 with the timestamp and do not lower any score here.
- A machine, object, ground mark, route, tree, water or wear that fails to carry across a cut, an idle
  or parked site, aimless or looped motion, cause and effect, and how much work a clip contains belong
  to Lens 3. If you see it, write SEE LENS 3 with the timestamp and do not lower any score here.
  Vegetation is read here only as a season carrier.
- The structure itself (identity, growth, less complete after a cut, a piece appearing finished)
  belongs to Lens 4. If you see it, write SEE LENS 4 with the timestamp and do not lower any score
  here. You may use "more structure" as the sign that time passed; you do not score how it grew.
- The final beat and its payoff, the hook, a loop, the cover and the sound bed belong to Lens 5. If
  you see it, write SEE LENS 5 with the timestamp and do not lower any score here.
- If the media does not let you decide a point, write NOT VISIBLE or NOT VERIFIABLE. These are not
  zeros; a category that cannot be decided at all is NOT VERIFIABLE on its SCORES line.
- EVIDENCE RULE. A category score above 7 requires at least one timestamp (m:ss.s) and the visible
  thing at that timestamp that shows the category holding. "No defect found" is not evidence.
  Without it, the highest score for that category is 7.
- Each reversal is scored once: inside a clip in Category 1, a season out of order in Category 2, any
  other backwards cut in Category 6, in the final seconds in Category 8.
- A conditional whose condition is not met is NOT TRIGGERED: no score, and it enters no average.
  A triggered conditional is scored. Timestamps are m:ss.s on this file. Write no AVERAGE line.

## CATEGORY 1 — SUN AND SHADOW
Inside each clip, does the sun move one way, and do shadow length and the look of the light state the
same sun height? They are one fact stated twice; they must agree.
Look at: shadow direction at the start and end of every clip and the sweep between. Pick an object
whose height you can estimate (a fence, a storey, a machine): sun elevation = arctan(object height /
shadow length), at the start and end of every clip, into the SHADOW TABLE. Check it against the
light: hard short shadows with a high sun, long soft raking shadows with a low sun.
Defects: a shadow that reverses, jumps to a new angle, or stops while other motion continues; two
objects in one frame casting different directions or implying different sun heights; a long shadow
under a high sun; a short shadow when colour and softness say dawn or dusk; shadow length changing
while the sun's height does not. Barely moving shadows are a held hour, not a defect by themselves.
10  every clip: one sweep direction, length agreeing with the light; measurement named at m:ss.s
 7  one minor exception (a brief hesitation, one object slightly off), or no timestamped evidence
 5  one clear failure a viewer would notice once: a reversal, a jump or a contradicting shadow
 3  failures in two or more clips, or one reversal that breaks the day's arc
 0  shadows contradict the sun throughout

## CATEGORY 2 — SEASON ORDER AND CARRIERS
Across the clips, does the season move one way, and is each season shown by something a viewer can
point at rather than asserted by colour grading?
Look at: each clip's season and carrier: snow, foliage, bare branches, crop colour, ground condition,
clothing bulk, the sun's midday height. On a site with no vegetation or weather (desert, rock): the
boundary between pale cured concrete and the dark freshly poured slab, and the sun's height. A grade
alone is no carrier. Shadow length at a similar hour grows toward winter and shrinks toward summer;
each full long-short swing across the clips is one year. Foliage must match its own clip's sun height.
Defects: a season that runs backwards (winter before autumn, snow after the thaw, an earlier season
returning); a clip readable only from the grade; foliage contradicting its clip's sun height. One or
two seasons is not a defect; four seasons in scrambled order is.
10  every clip has a named carrier and the order runs one way; carriers named at m:ss.s
 7  one clip readable only from the grade, or no timestamped evidence
 5  one season out of order, or two clips with no carrier
 3  the order breaks more than once, or most clips have no carrier
 0  seasons scrambled throughout

## CATEGORY 3 — SKY AND CLOUD
Does the sky behave like one sky over one site: clouds streaking one consistent way, and cloud cover
agreeing with the light on the ground?
Look at: cloud direction in every clip; long smooth streaks or mere drift; cloud cover against shadow
hardness; sky colour against the hour the shadows show. Cloud speed is Category 4; exposure is 5.
Defects: clouds moving different ways in different clips with no weather change to explain it; cloud
direction turning inside a clip; hard dark shadows under full overcast, or a clear sunny sky over
shadowless ground; a dusk-coloured sky over noon shadows.
10  one sky throughout: one streak direction, cover and shadows agreeing; named at m:ss.s
 7  one minor inconsistency, or no timestamped evidence
 5  one clip where the sky contradicts the ground light, or direction flips with no weather change
 3  two or more such clips
 0  the sky behaves independently of the site throughout

## CATEGORY 4 — ONE CLOCK
Inside each clip, do ground and sky run on one clock at one steady rate? A clip shows one stretch of
time (normally a day) at one compression; the season changes between clips, not inside one.
Look at: the rate carriers side by side: shadow sweep, cloud streaks, people and vehicles (blurs and
streaks, not walkers), machine cycles (crane slew, bucket cycle); each clip's first half against its
second half. Weather within a day (a first snowfall, a shower) is not a season change.
Defects: shadows sweeping under still or crawling clouds, or clouds racing over still shadows; a
static sky over a time-lapsed ground; a passage dropping to real time; a sudden acceleration with no
cut; cloud speed changing inside a clip; people or vehicles at a plausible walking or driving pace
while shadows sweep; two clocks in one clip (a day passes and foliage turns, or snow comes and goes).
10  every clip runs one rate in sky and ground; matched carriers named at m:ss.s
 7  one minor mismatch, or no timestamped evidence
 5  one clip where sky and ground plainly disagree, or the rate changes once
 3  mismatches in two or more clips, or one clip running two clocks
 0  no clip keeps a single clock

## CATEGORY 5 — EXPOSURE STABILITY
Inside each clip, do brightness and colour change only when the light gives a reason? Flicker is the
classic artefact of this format.
Look at: brightness and colour temperature across each clip; the sky against the ground; the frame
edges. A new day after a cut may look different; across a cut, judge only same hour, same weather.
Defects: brightness pulsing from sample to sample; an exposure step with no cloud to explain it;
colour temperature swinging back and forth; the sky changing exposure independently of the ground;
breathing vignetting; two clips at the same hour and weather in very different colour.
10  steady in every clip except where the light explains a change; named at m:ss.s
 7  one faint pulse or step, or no timestamped evidence
 5  one visible flicker, step or colour swing a viewer would notice once
 3  flicker or steps in two or more clips
 0  the film flickers throughout

## CATEGORY 6 — THE CUT MEANS TIME ⭐
The camera never moves, so the cut is the only editorial act, and it must mean one thing: time
passed. At each cut in CUT TIMESTAMPS, can a first-time viewer tell that time passed, and that it went
forward? If a cut means "here is another shot", the film is a slideshow with a locked camera.
Look at: the last second before and the first second after each cut. Name in one phrase what tells
the viewer time passed: more structure, a stage change, a season change, the sun's yearly height, a
weather change. Then read the direction from those cues.
Defects: a cut where you cannot name that thing, so it reads as "next shot"; a cut across which
nothing changed; a cut that reads as a change of place, not of date, while the camera did not move;
a cut going backwards for a reason other than season or structure (the light rewinds while nothing
else changes, or the frame returns to an earlier clip's state: a replay). One caps this at 4.
10  every cut reads as later and forward; the time-passed sign named at each cut timestamp
 7  every cut reads as time but one is weak (faint, found only on a second look), or no evidence
 5  one cut reads as "next" or communicates no time
 3  two or more cuts read as "next", or one cut goes backwards
 0  the cuts work as separators between shots throughout

## CATEGORY 7 — JUMP SIZE AND WHAT SURVIVES THE CUT
Can the viewer feel roughly how much time each jump covers, and does each cut leave enough of the
frame unchanged that it reads as the same place, later?
Look at: at each cut, the size the carriers suggest (days, weeks, months, a season, a year or more)
and whether they agree: season and sun height against the amount of new structure, ground and sky
change. Then the balance: when most of the frame holds and a legible part changes, the eye reads
elapsed time; when nearly everything changes at once (light, sky, season, ground cover, structure),
the eye reads a new place although the camera did not move.
Defects: a cut whose size cannot be read; carriers disagreeing at one cut (the season says half a
year, structure and ground say a few days, or the reverse); a cut where so much changes that it
reads as another place. Equal jump sizes are not a defect. One object failing to carry: SEE LENS 3.
10  every cut has a readable size, agreeing carriers and a holding frame; named at m:ss.s
 7  one cut slightly hard to size, or no timestamped evidence
 5  one cut unreadable in size, with disagreeing carriers, or reading as another place
 3  two or more such cuts
 0  no cut can be sized, and the cuts read as changes of place

## CATEGORY 8 — THE LAST SECONDS
Do the final seconds bring time to rest, so a viewer knows the film is over rather than expecting
another shot? FINAL HOLD is the measured fact for how the file ends; do not re-measure it.
Look at: whether motion calms and the light comes to rest before the end; whether the stretch FINAL
HOLD names reads as a deliberate rest or as a playback stall (clouds and shadows stopped mid-streak,
a machine frozen mid-swing); whether the last seconds run backwards toward the clip's start. If FINAL
HOLD reports no static end, judge arrival against cut-off in mid-motion; length is never the defect.
Only if END CARD = YES, judge the hand-over: does the last scene settle before the card, and does the
card read as the film ending, not as one more stage of the build (and, if MUSIC = YES, arrive on a
musical resolution)? Do not judge what the card shows.
10  time comes to rest and the hold (and any card) reads as a deliberate end; named at m:ss.s
 7  reads as an end with one minor flaw (a slightly abrupt stop, a stray movement), or no evidence
 5  reads once as a stop, not an arrival: a stall, a halt mid-sweep, or a card arriving as a stage
 3  time races into the last frame and simply stops, or the last seconds run backwards
 0  nothing signals an end; a viewer expects another shot

## CONDITIONAL CATEGORIES
**C1 — RHYTHM AND PATTERN.** Open only if the facts block lists clips of unequal length (more than
one value in CLIP LENGTH ON FILE). Vertical films are a fixed equal grid by rule; equal lengths are
declared, not a defect: write NOT TRIGGERED here and on the rhythm verdict, and nothing else on
rhythm. When triggered: do the cuts make an arc (settling, building, peaking, releasing), or a
metronome? Is there a pattern-breaker, a cut landing differently because the clip before it earned a
pause or a rush? Is the peak on a cut or buried mid-clip? Does the last cut feel like an arrival?
Weight can sit in what happens just before each cut: judge the felt rhythm, not the intervals.
10 arc, pattern-breaker, peak on a cut, at its timestamp · 7 arc, no clear pattern-breaker, or no
evidence · 5 faint arc, most cuts alike · 3 interchangeable cuts (cap 4) · 0 no arc, peak mid-clip

**C2 — MUSIC ALIGNMENT.** Open only if MUSIC = YES. Does each cut land on a beat or bar line, or
mid-phrase? Does the loudest or widest musical moment meet the most important cut? Write ON THE BEAT /
MID-PHRASE / NOT AUDIBLE in the CUT TABLE; you cannot measure offsets, so write none.
10 every cut on the beat, the peak on the key cut, at m:ss.s · 7 one cut mid-phrase · 5 several
mid-phrase, or the peak off the key cut · 3 cuts and music unrelated · 0 music works against the cuts

**C3 — NIGHT OR DUSK.** Open only if any clip shows dusk or night. Do lights come on progressively,
floor by floor from the bottom up, not all at once? Is the night sky free of star trails and streaks?
Do dusk colour and long shadows arrive together? Lit signs, neon, window content: SEE LENS 1.
10 progressive lights, clean sky, dusk colour with long shadows, at m:ss.s · 7 one minor flaw · 5 all
lights at once, or one streaked sky · 3 two or more such defects · 0 night contradicts the day's light

**C4 — SNOW AND RAIN.** Open only if snow or rain is visible in any clip. Snow arrives, sits and melts
in order; snow appearing or vanishing without accumulating or melting, or melting and returning in
one clip, is a defect. Rain darkens surfaces, which then dry, in that order; standing water forms
where the ground is lowest and drains or evaporates, never simply vanishes. Across a cut: SEE LENS 3.
10 every event ordered, at m:ss.s · 7 one minor slip · 5 one event out of order or vanishing · 3 two
or more · 0 weather comes and goes at random

**C5 — SEASON TURNS AT A CUT.** Open only if the season visibly changes at any cut. Order is Category
2; here judge how the turn is made. Does the outgoing clip prepare it (first snow, first thaw, first
turning leaves), or does the season switch without warning? An in-between state is a picture, not a
phrase: no first sign before the cut and no in-between state on either side means unprepared.
10 every turn prepared, the sign at m:ss.s · 7 one turn faintly prepared · 5 one unprepared turn ·
3 two or more unprepared · 0 every season arrives as a switch

**C6 — VANTAGE CHANGE.** Open only if a cut moves to a different vantage. The change and the frame's
anchoring are Lens 1: write SEE LENS 1 and do not score them here. Judge only time: do the other
vantage's sun and season sit in order between its neighbours, does the film return to the main
vantage, and does the cut back read as later rather than "back to the first shot"?
10 the excursion sits in the time flow, the return reads as later, at m:ss.s · 7 one weak point ·
5 the return reads as a rewind · 3 the excursion breaks the time order · 0 time is lost across it

How to answer the verdict lines:
- ONLY LIGHT: the changes could come from re-grading a static scene; nothing visibly takes time.
- WRONG DIRECTION: the world changes but the arrow breaks (a season returns, a cut runs backwards, a
  shadow reverses). Mandatory when a reversal is why two or more of Categories 1, 2 and 6 score
  below 4, however convincing the overall progression looks.
- PASSING: only when time moves, and moves forward, throughout. NEXT: the cuts work as separators
  between shots rather than as jumps forward in time. METRONOME: every cut lands with the same weight.
- THE CUT THAT CARRIES THE FILM: the one cut whose jump a viewer feels most; NONE if no cut does.
- TIME READ FROM THE FILM ALONE: from the film alone, as an order of magnitude. Do not hedge or
  decline; if it is far from what was intended, the film is not telling its own span.
- The worse answer wins: a bad verdict stands against good scores. PASSING with Category 1 or 2 at 3
  or below, or TIME with Category 6 at 3 or below, is inconsistent: re-watch and correct one of them.

QUESTIONS FOR THE DOCUMENT
Numbered questions on what the film alone could not settle. Ask; do not answer. Always ask for the
declared sun direction and height per clip, the declared season per clip, the declared elapsed time
per cut and for the whole film, and any clip planned as a held hour; if MUSIC = YES, the declared
tempo; if C6 is triggered, whether the vantage change was planned. Add every NOT VERIFIABLE point.
